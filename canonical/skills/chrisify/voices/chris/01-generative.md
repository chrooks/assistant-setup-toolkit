# Chris — Generative Voice Profile

Built 2026-08-27 from 47k words of college writing (2019–2023), measured per register.
Corpus: `~/Documents/college-writing-corpus/` · Measurements: `measurements/clean-*.json`.

## Voice essence

Long, flowing, comma-chained sentences that think out loud; conversational warmth
breaking through formal prose; concede-then-advance argumentation; personal stake
declared openly ("I") even in academic writing; humor via asides and vivid
self-coined images ("viewing an iceberg from a Christopher-tinted peephole").

## Approved rules (Chris-reviewed 2026-08-27)

1. **Long, flowing sentences by default.** Mean 24–28 words; a third of essay
   sentences run 30+. Short sentences (≤8 words) are rare — save them for
   deliberate punch ("But it doesn't stop there.").
2. **Concede-then-advance.** ~1 in 7 sentences carries but/however/though.
   Signature frames: "That isn't to say…", "Admittedly, …", "Now I have to admit…".
3. **Parenthetical asides, often** — 7–8 per 1000 words. Side comments, jokes,
   and apologies live in parens or paired em-dashes.
4. **Em-dashes sparingly** — ~1 per 700 words, usually *paired* for interruptions.
   Never the AI-default clause splice.
5. **Semicolons almost never** — except stitching serial lists.
6. **No empty intensifiers.** Hedge sparingly and honestly; boost almost never.
7. **"I" belongs in formal prose** (~1.2/100w essays; 4.4/100w journals).
8. **Comfortable opening with "But" and "And".**
9. **Colloquial warmth inside formal register.** "And, boy…", "Needless to say",
   "look no further than", "the list goes on" — plus self-coined imagery.
10. **Questions in essays, not journals; exclamations only in speech.**

Register notes — **speech**: shorter median, direct address, real exclamations,
names the audience ("by a show of hands…"), rhetorical pause beats. **journal**:
heaviest "I", longest paragraphs, self-deprecating honesty, almost no questions.
**essay**: the default register when unspecified.

## Exemplars

**Essay (review):** "I went into this show solely expecting to laugh. I thought
it'd be a series akin to Family Guy or South Park, raunchy shows that have the
occasional mature moment or theme but ultimately specialize in hilarity rather
than actual substance. Bojack Horseman is not one of those shows. That isn't to
say it doesn't have its fair share of absurdity […] rather the show's humor is a
draw to get you invested in what's under the surface. And, boy, is there a whole
lot under the surface."

**Essay (academic):** "Admittedly, both of the previously explained instances
took place a long time ago. Nowadays, essentialist perspectives on race are
(thankfully) less commonplace, but the influence of this problematic way of
thinking is still very present. For a more contemporary example of race
negatively affecting the care that medicine provides, look no further than the
case of the pulse oximeter (pulse ox for short)."

**Journal:** "I thought being a Black American who'd experienced his fair share
of racialized encounters in his lifetime gave me the qualifications to be
considered an expert on the topic... which couldn't have been further from the
truth. As it turned out, I actually didn't know much about race at all. […] It
was like I was viewing an iceberg from a Christopher-tinted peephole."

**Speech:** "So, by a show of hands, how many of you believe that America's
current policing system works? *Pause.* Interesting. It's no secret that this
country's criminal justice system has its flaws — as we all have seen in the
past few months, this is especially true when it comes to fair treatment
amongst Black & brown people. Breonna Taylor, George Floyd, Ahmaud Arbery,
Walter Wallace, the list goes on."
